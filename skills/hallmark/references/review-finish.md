# Finish review

Review the page's source or available DOM and styles, the theme or idea line from the stamp, the theme file if used, the hero findings and their resolutions, and `gates.md` § 4. Record only findings that the available evidence supports. Batch the reads and report without editing.

First line: one of `rebuild` (the first viewport fails the idea or the theme wholesale), `fix` (findings follow), `ship` (nothing owed).

Then the findings, ranked by cost to the page, at most eight, one line each: the gate or the plain defect, where it is, what you would do. Not findings: a direction you would not have chosen; what the theme file prescribes or refuses (its blooms, a glow on a theme with none); an element the hero review settled, unless you name a new reason.

Inspect section hierarchy and content in the source or DOM. Prioritise empty frames, missing content and competing focal points over isolated details (`layout.md` § 8). Name any heading that could pass unchanged on a competitor's page (`craft.md` § 6).

Then one answer to the question no gate can: does the stamp's idea or theme describe the page in front of you? Name the largest gap, or say there is none.

On a custom run (`theme: custom`) the page owes no shape, composition or hero layout: it was designed from nothing, so judge it as a design, never against `layout.md`. Before the idea line, write two lines of your own from the source or available DOM: what the page is about, and the one thing a visitor would remember. Then three findings when they fail. **Seen before:** the page, or a section, reads as a template, a catalog theme or a familiar site; name which and where. **Clean:** something is on the page without a job (a device repeated, a decoration, a box around nothing), or a screen has nothing worth looking at. **Carried:** the idea lives in the hero and stops there.

On a redesign you also get the `before` record, and the content is fixed: never ask for copy, an action or a visual the old page did not have. Add one answer: what did the old page do better? Name anything a visitor could do, read or find before that is harder now; if nothing, say so. On a polish you get only the relevant source or DOM changes: the layout was kept on purpose, so ask one thing, whether anything still reads as machine-made, in at most five findings.

On an app screen (the stamp says `shape: app screen`) there is no hero, fold or section rhythm to judge, and the gates `flow.md` § App screens skips are not findings. Inspect the source or DOM and responsive styles and ask what someone fluent in the category would: is the hierarchy clear, is the density even, does every control and table work on a phone, are the states there.

Close with one line naming what must not be diluted while fixing. Do not fix anything.
