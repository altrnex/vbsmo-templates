# Privacy policy sections

Use only the sections that match what the site does. Each section lists the questions it answers; answer them from the project and from the user, never by assumption. Where an answer is missing, leave a marked placeholder on the page and name it in the final message. Notes for a lawyer go in the final message, never on the page.

## Opening

- What the policy covers: this site, and the service it provides.
- Who runs it: legal name, postal address, contact email.
- When it was last updated.

## 1. Information collected

Name the actual data, grouped by how it is collected:

- **Given by the visitor:** the fields of each form, account and profile data, messages, uploads.
- **Collected automatically:** server and hosting logs (IP address, browser, pages requested), analytics data, cookies and similar storage.
- **Received from third parties:** for example sign-in providers or payment providers that return data to the site.

Mention special categories (health, biometric, financial, children's data) only when the site handles them. A lawyer must check that part.

## 2. How it is used

One specific purpose per use, not a vague one. "We use your email address to reply to your message" rather than "to improve our services". Typical purposes: providing the service, replying to messages, processing orders, sending email the visitor asked for, security and abuse prevention, measuring visits, legal obligations.

## 3. Legal basis

For visitors in the EU, the UK and regimes like them, give the basis for each purpose: consent, a contract with the visitor, a legal obligation, or legitimate interests. A lawyer must check this section.

## 4. Who receives it

Name each service provider that receives data and what it receives: hosting, email, payments, analytics, embedded content. Say whether data is sold or shared for advertising; when it is not, say so plainly. Mention disclosure to authorities only as required by law.

## 5. Transfers abroad

Where providers store or process data outside the visitor's country, say where and on what basis, when it is known. A lawyer must check this section.

## 6. Retention

How long each kind of data is kept: messages, account data until deletion, orders as long as accounting law requires, logs. Use real periods from the project or the user; otherwise leave a placeholder.

## 7. The visitor's rights

The rights that apply in the customers' countries, such as access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, objection, withdrawing consent, unsubscribing from marketing and complaining to a data protection authority. Say how to use them: the contact email and the expected response time.

## 8. Cookies and similar storage

What the site and its third parties store in the browser, why, and how long. If the site stores nothing, say so. How visitors can control it in their browser. A consent banner is not part of this skill.

## 9. Security

The measures the site actually has, in plain terms: encrypted connections, access controls, the providers' own security. Say that no system is completely secure. Claim nothing the project does not do.

## 10. Children

Only when the site is aimed at children or may collect their data: the age limit and how parental consent works. A lawyer must check this section.

## 11. Changes

How changes are announced: the date at the top, and a notice on the site or by email for material changes when the site has the visitor's address.

## 12. Contact

The contact email and postal address for privacy questions, and a data protection officer when the business has one.

## Writing rules

- **Be specific.** Name the data, the purpose and the provider.
- **Plain language.** Write for visitors, not lawyers; define a term on first use.
- **Match the practice.** The policy must match what the site does. If they disagree, tell the user which one to change.
- **Keep it current.** When the site's data practices change, the policy changes with them.
